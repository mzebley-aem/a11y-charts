Indicator 5: Child Find (Birth to One)

Child Find is the process of identifying, locating, and evaluating infants and toddlers who may need early intervention services. Indicator 5 reports the percentage of infants from birth to age one who have an Individualized Family Service Plan (IFSP).

How is it measured?

Divide the number of infants from birth to age one with IFSPs by the state’s population of infants in that age range, then multiply by 100. Hawaii reports the statewide count; sampling is not used.

Quick Summary

Comparison to Target:

  • The FFY 2023 Child Find rate was 0.83%, which did not meet the 0.99% target. The rate decreased by 0.20 percentage points from 1.03% in FFY 2022.

Comparison to Baseline:

  • Hawaii selected FFY 2018 as the baseline year, when the Child Find rate was 0.85%. The FFY 2023 rate was 0.02 percentage points below the baseline.

How to read this chart

Actual values: The percentage of Hawaii infants from birth to age one with an IFSP.

Target values: Hawaii’s minimum annual Child Find goals, established with stakeholder input. An actual value meets the target when it is equal to or greater than the target.

Baseline value: Hawaii’s FFY 2018 starting point of 0.85%, used to assess improvement over time.

FFY 2023 result

Child Find (Birth to One) rate

Hawaii reported 125 infants from birth to age one with IFSPs out of an estimated population of 15,147. The resulting 0.83% rate was below both the 0.99% target and the prior year’s 1.03% rate.

Why did performance slip?

Slippage explanation

Hawaii experienced a decrease in births from 15,272 in FFY 2022 to 14,762 in FFY 2023; however, there was a slight increase in referrals from FFY 2022 and FFY 2023. In FFY 2023, less children were determined eligible. And of those found eligible, there was an increase in families who declined early intervention services even though their child was found eligible. Unfortunately, our current data system does not collect data on why families decline early intervention services. Furthermore, based on U.S. Census Bureau’s report, Hawaii’s population continues to fall in 2023, reflecting in a 0.3% population growth rate. It’s a decrease equivalent to 12 people per day.

Historical performance

Hawaii met or exceeded its Indicator 5 target in FFY 2019 and FFY 2022. The rate fell sharply in FFY 2020, recovered to 1.03% in FFY 2022, and then decreased to 0.83% in FFY 2023.
